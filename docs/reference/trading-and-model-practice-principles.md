# Trading and model practice principles (canonical list)

> **Doc status:** `unknown` · category `lookup` · last verified `never` · registered in [`docs/DOCUMENT-INDEX.md`](../DOCUMENT-INDEX.md) · **nobody has verified this document's status — do not act on it as current**

**What this is.** A short, numbered list of principles that skills point at. It is drawn from what real-world practice
and published evidence show. Every line cites the evidence behind it. The evidence lives in the dated practice reviews
(first: [`docs/research/practice-review-trade-mgmt-and-ai-2026-10-28.md`](../research/practice-review-trade-mgmt-and-ai-2026-10-28.md),
whose `[S#]` tags are used below). The weekly "Practice & methodology refresh" in
[`.claude/skills/frontier-research/SKILL.md`](../../.claude/skills/frontier-research/SKILL.md) keeps it current. It
edits this list in place, dates each change, and writes a new dated review.

**What this is not.** It is not a gate and not a config. It authorizes nothing. A principle that conflicts with a
binding operator rule (CLAUDE.md, `docs/CLAUDE-RULES-CANONICAL.md`, the TP doctrine, the soak doctrine) loses to that
rule. The conflict is then raised to the manager as a question; nobody resolves it by editing either side.

**Two research tracks** (operator decision 2026-10-28): **TRADING** covers how to trade better (strategies, entries,
exits, active management, sizing, costs, venues). **MODEL** covers how to build a better AI trader (data, labels,
features, training, evaluation, model families, infra, desk-as-teacher). **RESEARCH-OPS** applies to both.

## TRADING

- **T1.** Judge any in-trade intervention (human, LLM or rule) against **hold-to-exit AND a matched random intervention** (same count and type of actions at random checkpoints). Experts' exits often lose to random ones. [S1, S2, S3]
- **T2.** The value of a stop, trail or cut depends on the return process. It helps under momentum or regime switching and hurts under mean reversion. Evaluate it **per leg and per regime**, never pooled. [S4, S5, S30] *Strengthened 2026-10-28 for crypto: stop-loss momentum beat plain momentum across 147 coins, 2015–2022 [S62 ACAD, abstract].*
- **T3.** Evaluate every management rule **net of the full cost stack**. Cost is what turns tight stops from helpful into harmful. [S5, S6] *Strengthened 2026-10-28: for perpetuals the stack includes funding paid over the holding period, which changes the optimal exit schedule itself [S61 ACAD preprint, abstract].* *Strengthened 2026-11-04: at our per-leg n, a leg's own net estimate is mostly noise. Practice estimates the gross edge pooled (the leg, its asset class, all legs), shrinks it, and only then subtracts **that leg's own** cost, because costs are far more predictable than returns [S65 PRAC, full post].* *Strengthened 2026-11-11: measure execution cost as a **markout** (the fill against the midprice some time later, net of fees), not as spread or fee alone, and count order size against displayed depth and **forced exits** as cost channels. On the full Hyperliquid perp record, valued against the mid two hours later, aggressive orders that ignore displayed depth paid the most, liquidations exited far worse than any voluntary trade, and algorithmic takers only broke even [S74 ACAD, AFT 2026 extended abstract, full].*
- **T4.** Size stops and trails from **volatility and the intended holding period**, not from the entry price. A breakeven move has no special standing. [S9, S10, S11]
- **T5.** **Separate the check-in clock from the action rate.** Check in on the position's own bar and on events, and record "no action" as a decision. Large, information-driven actions add value; frequent small tweaks do not. [S12, S15]
- **T6.** Let **closed-list reasons** drive in-trade action: level broken, momentum reversal, volatility change against the position, thesis invalidated, time. Never act on salience of P&L alone. [S1, S15]
- **T7.** Put giveback limits at the **account or day level**. A per-trade giveback exit is a hypothesis to test, not established practice. [S16]
- **T8.** Isolate the exit's contribution with **fixed or random entries** before crediting management. [S17] *Strengthened 2026-11-11 (cautionary): replaying a leg's own trades under alternative exits is the right instrument, but a 900-trade crypto exit replay that favoured tighter stops, earlier targets and closer trails dropped its chronological split (the last regime was unusual) for a randomised one, with no selection deflation. Keep the split chronological and deflate the grid (M2, M5) [S75 ACAD preprint, abstract].*
- **T9.** Profit targets are justified for **mean-reversion** legs. For **trend** legs, practice lets a trail do the work. Under our TP doctrine, measure that cost; do not assume it. [S9, S25] *Strengthened 2026-11-04 (evidence only; the TP doctrine still binds): a survivorship-free crypto trend study of 21,616 assets exits only by a ratcheting channel-midpoint trail, with no profit target, and reports a positive net result at 0.10% cost per trade [S67 ACAD/PRAC, secondary summary]. Treat it as a measured arm.*
- **T10.** Partials and scaling out **reshape the distribution** of outcomes; the evidence does not show they add expectancy. Test them as arms, never as defaults. [S26]
- **T11.** *(added 2026-10-28 refresh)* **Size by forecast volatility, and expect its benefit to differ by asset.** Volatility targeting cut extreme returns and vol-of-vol in all 60+ assets tested. It raised Sharpe only for risk assets with a leverage effect (equities, credit) and was negligible for bonds, FX and commodities. Judge a vol-scaled sizing arm **per asset class**, on tails as well as Sharpe. [S56 ACAD/PRAC]
- **T12.** *(added 2026-10-28 refresh)* **Trade only when the target leaves a no-trade buffer.** Choose the buffer width on after-cost results, then widen it, because a backtest-optimal buffer is too narrow. The same logic caps how often in-trade management may act (T5). [S57 PRAC/VEND; S64 ACAD, secondary]
- **T13.** **Active management is the whole bracket, one action class at a time** (operator, binding, 2026-10-28: *"active management isn't only tightening - it can be any movement in either bracket triggered by developments in the live market data"*). The classes are SL tighten / widen / move-to-structure, TP extend / pull-in / revise, partial / full close, and hold. Each is triggered by live market data and leaves both brackets in place. Each graduates from advice-only to executed on its own registered, net-of-cost test under the risk-adjusted rule `RULE-AM-RISK-ADJUSTED-V1` (operator 2026-10-29). Under that rule, a small pre-stated R cost can buy a large cut in book risk, and the class must still beat a matched random intervention (T1). No class passes it on the landed records (re-grade 2026-10-29). This is a binding rule, and the canonical text is [`docs/ARCHITECTURE-CANONICAL.md`](../ARCHITECTURE-CANONICAL.md) § "Active management". Evidence for the hold baseline: [S1, S2, S3]
- **T14.** *(added 2026-11-04 refresh)* **Report the left tail beside the mean.** Every management or sizing verdict states a 10% or 25% quantile of outcome R and the max drawdown next to mean R, because rankings flip when the objective changes from mean or Sharpe to the tail. This is a reporting rule, not a new gate; `RULE-AM-RISK-ADJUSTED-V1` still decides. [S66 PRAC, summary; S68 ACAD preprint, abstract]

## MODEL

- **M1.** **Label with the path-dependent, cost-net outcome of the exit policy actually traded**: triple-barrier from each decision state, or the priced outcome of every action. A binary label or a gross label is not enough. [S50, S38]
- **M2.** **Validate with purged, embargoed walk-forward or combinatorial purged CV**, grouped by trade when several decision points share one price path. [S50]
- **M3.** **Control leakage** explicitly: point-in-time features, a hold-out after any pretrained model's release, and masked identifiers for LLM inputs. [S55, S53] *Strengthened 2026-11-04: leakage also runs through **temporal overlap of correlated series**, so the hold-out must start after the training data ends for every correlated symbol, not just the target. LLM look-ahead shows up as alpha decay across regimes; measure it there [S70 ACAD preprint, abstract; S71 ACAD preprint, abstract].*
- **M4.** **Pre-register the decision rule and the analysis plan** (transforms, windows, exclusions, strata). Count every configuration tried. [S20, S49]
- **M5.** **Deflate for selection**: deflated or probabilistic Sharpe, PBO, and a t-hurdle above 3 for new effects. Confirm on a holdout after the training period. *Amended 2026-11-05 (operator rule, HOLDOUT-DEFLATED-REUSE): a holdout may be re-read; every re-read is recorded and raises the bar — see M13.* [S45, S46, S47, S48]
- **M6.** **Beat random and simple-rule baselines at the same decision points**, net of cost. A gain in AUC or Brier score over a constant is feature lift, not edge. [S1, S20, S42] *Strengthened 2026-10-28: on a live, contamination-free benchmark most LLM agents posted poor returns and weak risk control [S53 AI-Trader, re-read at abstract level].*
- **M7.** **Stratify evaluation by regime** (trend and volatility). LLM and ML edges are regime-asymmetric. [S22, S4]
- **M8.** For any trade-management model, **evaluate the check-in cadence and the trigger set as factors**, with action-level and no-action lift reported separately. [S12, S1]
- **M9.** **Prefer a supervised meta-model over the best rule** to an end-to-end policy. Use RL only as fine-tuning initialized from it. Do not use pure imitation of a teacher's choices as the target. [S19, S38, S39]
- **M10.** **Promote on out-of-sample decision value net of cost** against the incumbent, the best rule and the random control. Agreement with a teacher never gates promotion. Monitor live decay with a CUSUM after promotion. [S1, S43, benchmark S14]
- **M11.** *(added 2026-10-28 refresh)* **Measure an LLM decision-maker's repeatability before grading its edge.** Re-run the same snapshot k ≥ 3 times. Report agreement on the final decision separately from agreement on reasoning or tool sequence. A recommendation that flips across reruns is noise, not a decision. LLM agents show large run-to-run variance even at temperature 0, and they flip actions between adjacent steps. In one replay harness, decisions repeated in 84% of cases, but tool sequences repeated in only 26%. [S58 ACAD preprint, abstract + snippet; S59 ACAD preprint, abstract] *Strengthened 2026-11-04: in live benchmarks, the agent **harness** (roles, risk step, prompt) moved behaviour more than the model did, and general LLM leaderboard scores did not predict trading results. A harness or brief change is therefore a treatment to re-test, like a model change [S72 ACAD preprint, abstract; S73 ACAD preprint, summary].* *Noted 2026-11-11: repeatability is necessary, not evidence of skill; decision determinism and task accuracy were uncorrelated (r = −0.11, n = 21) [S59 re-read, search summary].*
- **M12.** *(added 2026-11-04 refresh)* **Score a model per asset (or per leg) against the simple domain baseline, not on pooled loss, and recalibrate scale before crediting skill.** In a 50-asset comparison, the foundation models' pooled edge over HAR came from a few outlier assets. Per asset, only one small model beat Log-HAR, and narrowly. Much of the short-horizon gain was forecast scale, which Mincer–Zarnowitz recalibration removed. [S69 ACAD preprint, abstract] *Strengthened 2026-11-11: also check forecast **amplitude** and **cross-series ranking**. On 1,000 equities, TSFM return forecasts collapsed to near-flat with weak cross-sectional correlation, which per-series loss hid [S76 ACAD preprint, abstract]; five TSFMs beat a random walk significantly in only 2 of 10 Diebold–Mariano tests [S77 ACAD preprint, abstract].* *Scope note 2026-11-11: under the operator rule of 2026-11-04 (CLAUDE.md, "An account configures strategies, never symbols": per-symbol results never gate), M12's per-asset comparison decides what is **reported**, and the registered pooled test decides what trades. The tension is stated in `docs/plans/ML-ROADMAP-MECE-2026-11.md` and goes to the manager; neither side is edited here.*
- **M13.** *(added 2026-11-05, operator rule)* **Re-reading a holdout raises the bar; it is never refused.** Operator, verbatim (chat, 2026-11-05 ~19:05Z): *"if we've trained a model and it didn't perform and we know what we want to tweak, can we create a new copy that hasn't been trained on the data so that we can retrain it with the tweaks that we want and see if that makes a difference? We can't just, like, give up on a certain line of research because we've already trained a model on all the data that we have. That's ridiculous."* Popup answer, verbatim: *"Adopt (Rec.)"*. This replaces "one model, one read; a second variant needs a fresh window" (operator 2026-11-01, `ML-BAR-ONE-READ`). The rule:
  1. **Training-period walk-forward slices:** tweak and retrain without limit. These reads are not recorded.
  2. **Re-testing on the same holdout window is allowed.** Every read is recorded in `comms/research/holdout_reads.jsonl` (`scripts/research/holdout_lock.py`), with its family, variant, window and `n_tries`. The N-th read of a family on an overlapping window is graded against a **deflated bar**. CI bars use a Šidák-corrected interval (one-sided α_N = 1 − 0.975^(1/N); z = 1.96 at N = 1, 2.24 at N = 2, 2.57 at N = 5). Point bars ("mean ≥ 0") use the Bailey–López de Prado deflated-Sharpe hurdle: mean/SE ≥ E[max of N null t-stats], which is 0 at N = 1, 0.52 at N = 2 and 1.19 at N = 5. The first read is graded against the same bar as before.
  3. **A family may use several holdout windows**, all after its training period. A disjoint window starts its own count.
  4. **Never test on dates that overlap training** (`PI-20261105-GEXSLEAK-0001`, M3). `check_window_disjoint` refuses a claim whose start is on or before `train_end`. This includes a symbol holdout read over training dates. It is the ledger's one hard refusal.
  *Gates this adds or changes:* none added. This **removes** a block, the one-read refusal. The time-disjointness refusal already followed from M3. [operator 2026-11-05; S45, S46]

- **M14.** *(added 2026-11-11 refresh)* **Treat an LLM's own trading returns as a claim, not evidence, and prefer the LLM upstream of deterministic calibration, sizing, risk and execution.** A reported LLM-agent return supports a deployment claim only after six checks: temporal integrity (no contamination), real frictions, counterfactual robustness, calibrated probabilities (language confidence is not a probability), numerical execution, and a per-agent or per-role disaggregation. The practice-preferred design uses the LLM as an auditable information interface feeding independent calibration, risk and execution modules. A desk trading on the demo floor is Stage-1 mechanics (R3) and never edge. This is a reporting and design principle, not a gate. [S78 ACAD position preprint, abstract; consistent with S53, S72, S73]

## RESEARCH-OPS (both tracks)

- **R1.** Every design arm **names its practice precedent** (source and tag) or is declared `novel`. [operator 2026-10-28]
- **R2.** Run an **assembly line, not lone end-to-end builders**, and measure each station's quality on its own. [S18]
- **R3.** **A live or paper book establishes mechanics, not edge.** Treat history as having no true OOS and do not tweak models on live results. [S20, S40, S41] *Tension noted 2026-10-28: S20's "keep models simple" is weakened as stated, because shrunk high-parameter models out-predicted sparse ones on US equity returns [S63 ACAD, abstract + secondary]. What survives is "simple to validate": the OOS gates (M5, M10) decide, not the parameter count. Nothing is removed.*
- **R4.** **Plan for decay.** Expect large out-of-sample and post-publication shortfalls. [S43]
- **R5.** **More backtests per idea means a larger live gap.** The count of backtests is an anti-metric. [S42] *Reconciled 2026-11-01 (lane RESEARCH-ITERATE) with R7: the anti-metric is UNCOUNTED, ad-hoc trial. Variants searched along a declared lever map are registered before the run, counted, deflated (M4, M5) and confirmed on a holdout. Each re-read of that holdout raises the bar (M13). That is the opposite of trial-and-error.*
- **R6.** **Refresh this list weekly from outside evidence**, and turn every gap it reveals into a checklist row or a queue unit. Never leave it as a memo. [frontier-research]
- **R7.** *(added 2026-11-01, operator rule)* **Understand how each lever moves the outcome before deciding anything.** Attribute every result first (P&L by entry quality, exit capture, filter effect and cost; by regime, year, symbol, direction, session), then measure each declared lever one factor at a time against a baseline control, then response surfaces where interactions are suspected. Keep the readings in a persistent per-subject lever response map (`research/levers/<subject>.yaml`). A smooth, fold-stable response on a lever is potential; a spiky single-point optimum is overfit; a flat or noisy response on every material lever is evidence of no edge. Operator, verbatim: *"we have to have a process here by which we understand how to test the different levers so that we can understand how each lever affects the outcome, and based on that, decide if something has potential or not have potential."* Canonical: OPERATING-PLAN § 2 "What does not pass goes back into research". [operator 2026-11-01; parameter-stability practice: prefer plateaus to peaks (Pardo, *The Evaluation and Optimization of Trading Strategies*, 2008); selection control S45–S48] *Strengthened 2026-11-04: an outside protocol prescribes the same sequence: stable regions, then walk-forward with a majority-pass rule and a catastrophic veto, then a locked OOS [S68 ACAD preprint, abstract].*
- **R8.** *(added 2026-11-01, operator rule)* **Retire only when the lever map is exhausted**, never on a count of attempts: every material lever family tested at adequate power, net of the full cost stack, across regimes, with none producing an edge (`scripts/research/family_retirement_policy.yaml` v3). Until then the subject iterates. [operator 2026-11-01]
