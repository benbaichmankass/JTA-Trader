## 3. Trade management: entry, in-trade, exit

Provenance: MEASURED

Pipeline stage(s): 7 Order + bracket, 8 Execution, 9 Live mgmt, 10 Close

1. **Entry** — Stop-loss defines 1R; profit target is a real prediction (the TP doctrine).
2. **In-trade** — Either bracket moves, part-close or close, triggered by live market evidence.
3. **Review desk** — Every open trade reviewed; options priced from the strategy's own history. 789 reviews on record.
4. **Exit + grade** — Close reason recorded, trade graded, grade becomes a training label.

> MEASURED: real-money target at entry 7 of 8 routings, revised in-trade 1 of 8 (scripts/ci/check_tp_doctrine.py); reviews: comms/live_review/reviews.jsonl. Rules: docs/ARCHITECTURE-CANONICAL.md § TP doctrine, § Active management.


**Speaker notes.** Steps 7 to 11 in detail, because this is where the trade is actually managed. At entry every order carries a protective stop, which defines 1R, and a profit target that must be a real prediction, not a placeholder set too far away to ever fill. In the trade, either bracket can move, or the position can be part-closed or closed, but only when something in the live market triggers it: structure, volatility, momentum, regime or a scheduled event. Never the profit alone, never the clock alone. A review function looks at every open trade, prices each option from that strategy's own backtest history, and since 14 November its recommended actions are applied automatically through each account's own risk checks, with a ping to the operator. At the exit the close reason is recorded and graded, and the grade feeds research. Honest status on targets: on real money 7 of 8 routings set a real target at entry, but only 1 of 8 revise it during the trade.

## 4. The R&D loop: how a model earns, and keeps, its place

Pipeline stage(s): 11 Grade + R&D

1. **Diagnose** — Why is the champion not better? Name the lever: label, features, threshold, exit.
2. **Pre-register** — Test, statistic and pass mark written down before the run.
3. **Test** — Held-out data, full cost stack, challenger vs champion on the same trades.
4. **Decide** — Pass → real money + mirror. Fail → back to Diagnose.
5. **Monitor** — Mirror net-negative after costs → demo floor, keep iterating.

> Rules: docs/plans/OPERATING-PLAN-2026-09-21.md § 2 (no step between a backtest pass and real money, 2026-11-12; the held-out pass governs real money) and § “What does not pass goes back into research”.


**Speaker notes.** This is the core of the project. Every strategy and every model sits in the same loop. Start from a diagnosis of why the current version is not better. Write down the test and its pass mark before running it. Run it on data held out from tuning, charged with the full cost of trading. Compare the challenger with the champion, the version in use, on the same trades and the same costs. Pass, and it goes straight to real money with a paper mirror. Fail, and the result is not thrown away: it becomes the next diagnosis. A live model whose mirror goes net-negative after costs drops back to the demo floor and keeps generating data while it is improved. Retirement needs evidence that no lever helps, never a fixed number of attempts.

## 5. One real cycle: the equity entry model, November 2026

Provenance: MEASURED

Pipeline stage(s): 3 Strategies + ML, 11 Grade + R&D

| Step | What happened | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Promote | Held-out confirmation, 173-fund pool, long only, net of costs | +0.49R per trade, n 207 — pass |
| Diagnose | Trades cluster in volatile months; label never tested on this pool | lever: the training label |
| Test (207f-03) | Does the score predict direction, or cost per R? | fail: score tracks cost per R |
| Evidence | Score vs cost per R; direction after removing volatility | IC −0.53; IC −0.01 (t −0.95) |
| Next (c21d-06) | Cost-neutral target vs the live recipe, same trade count | registered 2026-11-17, queued |

> MEASURED: research/queue/RQ-20261111-207f-03.yaml (graded fail 2026-11-17) and RQ-20261117-c21d-06.yaml (queued). IC = rank correlation between the model's score and the quantity named, across decisions.


**Speaker notes.** A worked example of the loop on a model trading real money today. The equity entry model, internally Foxhound-4, passed its held-out confirmation and was promoted to real money on 8 November. Its diagnosis said it trades in clusters during volatile months. So a test was pre-registered: does the model really predict direction, or is it ranking trades by how cheap they are to trade relative to their risk? The answer, graded on 17 November, was the second: its score tracks cost per unit of risk, and once volatility is neutralised it has almost no directional signal. The test's two alternative labels did not beat it, so it failed. That failure named the next lever: a cost-neutral training target, registered the same day. The live model keeps trading while the challenger is tested; nothing waits on a calendar.

## 7. Step 3 · Foxhound: how an entry model decides

Provenance: MEASURED

Pipeline stage(s): 3 Strategies + ML

1. **Reads** — ~20 inputs: returns 1h–1wk, volatility, range position.
2. **Trees** — 300 gradient-boosted trees (equity: 5-model average).
3. **Predicts** — Net R of a standard 2-stop / 3-target trade.
4. **Acts** — Score above threshold → trade. Else nothing.

> Crypto, live: +0.037R per trade, n 907, held out. Crypto v2: -0.12R, n 320, failed → paper. MEASURED: ml/served/ml_decision_entry_v1/model.json, ml/candidates/ml_entry_eq_v3_struct_ens5/model.json, ml/served/SERVED_MODELS.yaml.


**Speaker notes.** Every four hours, for each market, the model reads about twenty numbers on recent moves, volatility and range position, and predicts the net R of a standard trade: stop two average ranges away, target three, held up to a day. Above a threshold it trades; otherwise it does nothing. The crypto model learned from about 660 thousand past decisions on 60 coins. The equity model averages five models on 173 funds, long only. Not every challenger wins: the second crypto version failed and stays on paper.

## 8. Step 4 · Collie and Beagle: when to step aside

Provenance: MEASURED

Pipeline stage(s): 4 Intent layer

1. **Collie reads** — Returns, volatility, hour, weekday + 6 forecast features.
2. **Collie decides** — Regime class → strategy steps aside where it loses.
3. **Beagle reads** — 9 trend and volatility features per signal.
4. **Beagle decides** — Weak signal → halve size. Strong → take it.

> Beagle no-harm test: n 1830, pass. Inventory 2026-11-11: 106 models; keep 15, review 19, retire 72. MEASURED: ml/overlay/overlay_td.json, ml/configs/btc-regime-15m-lgbm-fc-pcv-v2.yaml, comms/ml_consolidation/.


**Speaker notes.** Two families act between a proposal and an order. Collie classifies the market regime, calm or volatile, trending or not, from returns, volatility, time of day and six forecast features from a small pretrained forecaster; a strategy can be switched off only in the regimes where it loses. Beagle scores each signal from two trend strategies and halves the size of weak ones; it passed a no-harm test on 1,830 historical trades. An 11 November inventory proposed retiring most registered models that nothing uses: the loop pruning itself.

## 9. Steps 7 and 9 · Labrador: managing the open trade

Provenance: MEASURED

Pipeline stage(s): 7 Order + bracket, 9 Live mgmt

1. **Check-in** — At 5 points in the trade, read 29 inputs.
2. **Classify** — Gradient-boosted classifier over 18 target levels.
3. **Compare** — Expected net R of each level vs the current target.
4. **Act** — Clearly better → extend the target. Never pull it in.

> Target revision: +0.0008R per trade vs a fixed target, n 907. Exit head: -0.0141R per trade, n 1041, fail → demo floor. MEASURED: ml/served/ml_entry_tp_revision_v1/model.json, ml/served/SERVED_MODELS.yaml.


**Speaker notes.** Labrador re-predicts where a live trade is likely to get to at five check-ins, choosing among 18 target levels, and may only extend the target, never pull it in. Its measured gain is small but positive. The exit head decides whether to close now; it failed out of sample on two of three markets, so it closes trades only on the demo floor. Showing the small and failed results is part of the discipline.

## 13. How it runs automatically, in one slide

Provenance: MEASURED

Pipeline stage(s): 1 Data in, 2 Signals, 5 Routing, 8 Execution, 10 Close

- **Venues** — Crypto perpetual futures, US funds and futures, and prop-firm accounts. 11 of 14 accounts sending orders.
- **Two switches** — Account: live or dry run. Strategy: live or shadow. No hidden third switch; never self-halts.
- **Run by agents** — AI agent sessions do the research and reviews; 23 operator-granted mandates let evidence-backed decisions proceed.

> MEASURED: config/accounts.yaml, config/mandates.yaml; rules docs/CLAUDE-RULES-CANONICAL.md § Prime Directive, CLAUDE.md § The two execution gates; config/values_exclusions.yaml.


**Speaker notes.** The infrastructure, briefly. The trader runs around the clock on cloud servers, on crypto, US funds and futures venues, plus prop-firm accounts. 11 of 14 accounts are switched on to send orders. Exactly two switches decide whether something trades: one per account, one per strategy. It never switches itself off: failures are contained to one trade or job and retried. Day to day, AI agent sessions run the research, reviews and bookkeeping under 23 standing mandates the operator has granted; agents may propose a mandate, only the operator grants one. Real money excludes fossil-fuel funds by the operator's policy.
