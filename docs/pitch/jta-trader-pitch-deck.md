## 3. Trade management: entry, in-trade, exit

Provenance: MEASURED

1. **Entry** — Stop-loss defines 1R; profit target is a real prediction (the TP doctrine).
2. **In-trade** — Either bracket moves, part-close or close, triggered by live market evidence.
3. **Review desk** — Every open trade reviewed; options priced from the strategy's own history. 789 reviews on record.
4. **Exit + grade** — Close reason recorded, trade graded, grade becomes a training label.

> MEASURED: real-money target at entry 8 of 9 routings, revised in-trade 1 of 9 (scripts/ci/check_tp_doctrine.py); reviews: comms/live_review/reviews.jsonl. Rules: docs/ARCHITECTURE-CANONICAL.md § TP doctrine, § Active management.


**Speaker notes.** Steps 7 to 11 in detail, because this is where the trade is actually managed. At entry every order carries a protective stop, which defines 1R, and a profit target that must be a real prediction, not a placeholder set too far away to ever fill. In the trade, either bracket can move, or the position can be part-closed or closed, but only when something in the live market triggers it: structure, volatility, momentum, regime or a scheduled event. Never the profit alone, never the clock alone. A review function looks at every open trade, prices each option from that strategy's own backtest history, and since 14 November its recommended actions are applied automatically through each account's own risk checks, with a ping to the operator. At the exit the close reason is recorded and graded, and the grade feeds research. Honest status on targets: on real money 8 of 9 routings set a real target at entry, but only 1 of 9 revise it during the trade.

## 4. The R&D loop: how a model earns, and keeps, its place

1. **Diagnose** — Why is the champion not better? Name the lever: label, features, threshold, exit.
2. **Pre-register** — Test, statistic and pass mark written down before the run.
3. **Test** — Held-out data, full cost stack, challenger vs champion on the same trades.
4. **Decide** — Pass → real money + mirror. Fail → back to Diagnose.
5. **Monitor** — Mirror net-negative after costs → demo floor, keep iterating.

> Rules: docs/plans/OPERATING-PLAN-2026-09-21.md § 2 (no step between a backtest pass and real money, 2026-11-12; the held-out pass governs real money) and § “What does not pass goes back into research”.


**Speaker notes.** This is the core of the project. Every strategy and every model sits in the same loop. Start from a diagnosis of why the current version is not better. Write down the test and its pass mark before running it. Run it on data held out from tuning, charged with the full cost of trading. Compare the challenger with the champion, the version in use, on the same trades and the same costs. Pass, and it goes straight to real money with a paper mirror. Fail, and the result is not thrown away: it becomes the next diagnosis. A live model whose mirror goes net-negative after costs drops back to the demo floor and keeps generating data while it is improved. Retirement needs evidence that no lever helps, never a fixed number of attempts.

## 5. One real cycle: the equity entry model, November 2026

Provenance: MEASURED

| Step | What happened | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Promote | Held-out confirmation, 173-fund pool, long only, net of costs | +0.49R per trade, n 207 — pass |
| Diagnose | Trades cluster in volatile months; label never tested on this pool | lever: the training label |
| Test (207f-03) | Does the score predict direction, or cost per R? | fail: score tracks cost per R |
| Evidence | Score vs cost per R; direction after removing volatility | IC −0.53; IC −0.01 (t −0.95) |
| Next (c21d-06) | Cost-neutral target vs the live recipe, same trade count | registered 2026-11-17, queued |

> MEASURED: research/queue/RQ-20261111-207f-03.yaml (graded fail 2026-11-17) and RQ-20261117-c21d-06.yaml (queued). IC = rank correlation between the model's score and the quantity named, across decisions.


**Speaker notes.** A worked example of the loop on a model trading real money today. The equity entry model, internally Foxhound-4, passed its held-out confirmation and was promoted to real money on 8 November. Its diagnosis said it trades in clusters during volatile months. So a test was pre-registered: does the model really predict direction, or is it ranking trades by how cheap they are to trade relative to their risk? The answer, graded on 17 November, was the second: its score tracks cost per unit of risk, and once volatility is neutralised it has almost no directional signal. The test's two alternative labels did not beat it, so it failed. That failure named the next lever: a cost-neutral training target, registered the same day. The live model keeps trading while the challenger is tested; nothing waits on a calendar.

## 7. Entry models (Foxhound): how they decide

Provenance: MEASURED

- **What it reads** — Returns over 1 hour to 1 week, volatility, average range, position in the recent range, volume. About 20 inputs per market.
- **What it predicts** — Net R of a standard bracket (2-range stop, 3-range target, up to 24 hours). Trades when the prediction clears a threshold, every 4 hours.
- **Results** — Crypto (live): +0.037R per trade, n 907, held out. Second crypto version: -0.12R, n 320 — failed, paper only.

> MEASURED: ml/served/ml_decision_entry_v1/model.json (LightGBM, 300 trees, 20 features, 662,930 training rows, 60-coin reference set); ml/candidates/ml_entry_eq_v3_struct_ens5/model.json (5-seed ensemble, 21 features, 173 funds); results ml/served/SERVED_MODELS.yaml.


**Speaker notes.** How the entry models work, in plain terms. Every four hours, for each market in its universe, the model looks at about twenty numbers describing recent price moves, volatility and where price sits in its recent range. It predicts how many R a standard trade would make, net of costs: a stop two average daily ranges away, a target three away, held up to a day. If the prediction clears a threshold, it trades; otherwise it does nothing. The crypto model learned from about 660 thousand past decisions on 60 coins and trades five of them. The equity model is an average of five models trained with different random seeds, on 173 funds, long only. Not every challenger wins: the second crypto version failed its forward read and trades only on paper.

## 8. Filter, in-trade and exit models: Beagle and Labrador

Provenance: MEASURED

- **Beagle · signal filter** — Logistic regression, 9 inputs. Halves size on weak signals for 2 trend strategies. No-harm test on n 1830 trades: pass.
- **Labrador · target revision** — Gradient-boosted classifier over 18 target levels, 29 inputs, 5 check-ins; extend-only. Gain vs fixed target: +0.0008R per trade, n 907.
- **Labrador · exit head** — Close-now scorer. Out of sample: -0.0141R per trade, n 1041 — fail. Demo floor only.

> MEASURED: ml/overlay/overlay_td.json, ml/served/ml_entry_tp_revision_v1/model.json; results ml/served/SERVED_MODELS.yaml (each entry names its committed source record).


**Speaker notes.** Three models act after a trade is proposed. The filter, Beagle, is a logistic regression on nine trend and volatility features; on two trend strategies it halves the size of signals it scores as weak. It passed a test that it does no harm on 1,830 historical trades. The target-revision model, Labrador, re-predicts where a live trade is likely to get to at five check-ins and may only extend the target, never pull it in; its measured gain is small and positive. The exit head decides whether to close now; it failed its out-of-sample test on two of three markets, so it only closes trades on the demo floor and annotates elsewhere. Being honest about the small and failed ones is part of the discipline.

## 9. Regime models (Collie): knowing when to step aside

Provenance: MEASURED

- **What it reads** — Recent returns and volatility, hour and weekday; newer heads add 6 forecast features from a frozen pretrained forecaster.
- **What it decides** — The market's regime, so a strategy can step aside in the regimes where it loses net of cost.
- **Inventory** — 106 registered models on 2026-11-11: keep 15, review 19, retire 72.

> MEASURED: ml/configs/btc-regime-15m-lgbm-fc-pcv-v2.yaml (LightGBM multiclass, regime label, forecast features from chronos-bolt-tiny); stages docs/reference/model_names.yaml; inventory comms/ml_consolidation/2026-11-11.json (dated snapshot).


**Speaker notes.** The largest family by count. A regime model classifies the current market state, for example calm or volatile, trending or not, every 5 minutes to every day depending on the head. It reads recent returns, volatility and time of day, and the newest versions add six forecast features from a small pretrained time-series forecasting model. A strategy that loses money in a given regime can be switched off in that regime only. Today three heads are advisory on paper books and most others are in shadow, scoring without acting. An inventory on 11 November found most registered models had no consumer and proposed retiring them, which is the loop pruning itself.

## 13. How it runs automatically, in one slide

Provenance: MEASURED

- **Venues** — Crypto perpetual futures, US funds and futures, and prop-firm accounts. 11 of 14 accounts sending orders.
- **Two switches** — Account: live or dry run. Strategy: live or shadow. No hidden third switch; never self-halts.
- **Run by agents** — AI agent sessions do the research and reviews; 23 operator-granted mandates let evidence-backed decisions proceed.

> MEASURED: config/accounts.yaml, config/mandates.yaml; rules docs/CLAUDE-RULES-CANONICAL.md § Prime Directive, CLAUDE.md § The two execution gates; config/values_exclusions.yaml.


**Speaker notes.** The infrastructure, briefly. The trader runs around the clock on cloud servers, on crypto, US funds and futures venues, plus prop-firm accounts. 11 of 14 accounts are switched on to send orders. Exactly two switches decide whether something trades: one per account, one per strategy. It never switches itself off: failures are contained to one trade or job and retried. Day to day, AI agent sessions run the research, reviews and bookkeeping under 23 standing mandates the operator has granted; agents may propose a mandate, only the operator grants one. Real money excludes fossil-fuel funds by the operator's policy.
