## 3. Trade management: entry, in-trade, exit

Provenance: MEASURED

Pipeline stage(s): 7 Order package + bracket, 8 Venue execution, 9 Live management, 10 Close and reconcile

1. **Entry** — Stop-loss defines 1R; profit target is a real prediction (the TP doctrine).
2. **In-trade** — Either bracket moves, part-close or close, triggered by live market evidence.
3. **Review desk** — Every open trade reviewed; options priced from the strategy's own history. 789 reviews on record.
4. **Exit + grade** — Close reason recorded, trade graded, grade becomes a training label.

> MEASURED: real-money target at entry 7 of 8 routings, revised in-trade 1 of 8 (scripts/ci/check_tp_doctrine.py); reviews: comms/live_review/reviews.jsonl. Rules: docs/ARCHITECTURE-CANONICAL.md § TP doctrine, § Active management.


**Speaker notes.** Steps 7 to 11 in detail, because this is where the trade is actually managed. At entry every order carries a protective stop, which defines 1R, and a profit target that must be a real prediction, not a placeholder set too far away to ever fill. In the trade, either bracket can move, or the position can be part-closed or closed, but only when something in the live market triggers it: structure, volatility, momentum, regime or a scheduled event. Never the profit alone, never the clock alone. A review function looks at every open trade, prices each option from that strategy's own backtest history, and since 14 November its recommended actions are applied automatically through each account's own risk checks, with a ping to the operator. At the exit the close reason is recorded and graded, and the grade feeds research. Honest status on targets: on real money 7 of 8 routings set a real target at entry, but only 1 of 8 revise it during the trade.

## 4. The R&D loop: how a model earns, and keeps, its place

Pipeline stage(s): 11 Grading, evidence, research

1. **Diagnose** — Why is the champion not better? Name the lever: label, features, threshold, exit.
2. **Pre-register** — Test, statistic and pass mark written down before the run.
3. **Test** — Held-out data, full cost stack, challenger vs champion on the same trades.
4. **Decide** — Pass → real money + mirror. Fail → back to Diagnose.
5. **Monitor** — Mirror net-negative after costs → demo floor, keep iterating.

> Rules: docs/plans/OPERATING-PLAN-2026-09-21.md § 2 (no step between a backtest pass and real money, 2026-11-12; the held-out pass governs real money) and § “What does not pass goes back into research”.


**Speaker notes.** This is the core of the project. Every strategy and every model sits in the same loop. Start from a diagnosis of why the current version is not better. Write down the test and its pass mark before running it. Run it on data held out from tuning, charged with the full cost of trading. Compare the challenger with the champion, the version in use, on the same trades and the same costs. Pass, and it goes straight to real money with a paper mirror. Fail, and the result is not thrown away: it becomes the next diagnosis. A live model whose mirror goes net-negative after costs drops back to the demo floor and keeps generating data while it is improved. Retirement needs evidence that no lever helps, never a fixed number of attempts.

**Speaker notes.** A worked example of the loop on a model trading real money today. The equity entry model, internally Foxhound-4, passed its held-out confirmation and was promoted to real money on 8 November. Its diagnosis said it trades in clusters during volatile months. ## 7. Model card · Foxhound, the entry model (step 3)

Provenance: MEASURED

Pipeline stage(s): 3 Strategy legs and ML heads

**Why it's good at its job.** It scans every market every four hours and asks one question: after costs, would a standard trade here pay? If not, it stays out. Like a scout who has studied 660,000 past situations, it learns patterns in how prices move, not rules someone wrote down. It is judged only on data it never saw while learning.

| Stat | What | Provenance · kind · n / window |
|---|---|---|
| +0.037R | Crypto model, average result per trade | MEASURED · offline, held-out · n 907 · 2025-10 → 2026-09 |
| +0.49R | Equity model, per trade (mostly a cost effect) | MEASURED · offline, held-out · n 207 · 2025-07 → 2026-10 |
| +0.0018R | Refit vs old model (−0.092R) on recent weeks | MEASURED · offline, 14-day grade · n 281 · 2026-10-23 → 11-05 |
| −0.068R | Wider test, 51 coins at own costs: FAIL | MEASURED · offline, held-out · n 12,746 decisions · year to 2026-10 |

**What's next.** → Test a stricter trigger on the held-out year: it won on development data (6 of 8 periods). → Retrain the equity model so it predicts direction, not just cheapness to trade. → Widen to new coins only where each coin's own costs still leave a profit.


**Speaker notes.** Foxhound answers the first question of every trade: is there one worth taking right now? Think of a scout that has studied hundreds of thousands of past situations and only signals when the odds after costs look good; most of the time it says nothing. The crypto version trades real money on its held-out result. The equity version showed a much larger held-out result, but our own follow-up found it mostly picks trades that are cheap to make relative to their risk, so the next challenger targets that. A broader test on 51 coins at each coin's own costs failed, which is why it trades only the five it passed on.

## 8. Model card · Beagle, the signal filter (step 4)

Provenance: MEASURED

Pipeline stage(s): 4 Intent layer

**Why it's good at its job.** A second opinion on every trend signal: if the setup looks weak, it halves the bet instead of refusing it. Like a volume knob, not an off switch, so a wrong call costs little. Nine plain inputs (trend strength, volatility, distance from average), kept simple on purpose.

| Stat | What | Provenance · kind · n / window |
|---|---|---|
| PASS | No-harm test vs taking every signal | MEASURED · offline, out-of-sample · n 1830 trades · 2020-07 → 2026-10 |
| 2 legs | Live: halves size on weak signals | MEASURED · live, mechanics only · config/overlay_policy.yaml |
| NOT A WIN | First challenger vs Beagle | MEASURED · offline · graded indeterminate · 2026-11-16 |

**What's next.** → Build a challenger that disagrees with Beagle more often: new inputs or a profit target, not a new cutoff. → A live side-by-side read of the challenger runs in the background until enough disagreements pile up. → Regime models: keep only those that beat a simple baseline (72 of 106 proposed for retirement).


**Speaker notes.** Beagle sits between a strategy's signal and the order. It does not veto; it turns the volume down on signals that look weak, halving their size, on two trend strategies. It is deliberately simple, a logistic regression on nine inputs, because simple models are harder to fool themselves with. It passed a test that it does no harm across 1,830 historical trades. Its first challenger did not beat it, so the next one has to disagree with it more often to be worth testing. The regime family, Collie, also sits at this step; an inventory on 11 November proposed retiring most of its unused models.

## 9. Model card · Labrador, the in-trade manager (steps 7 and 9)

Provenance: MEASURED

Pipeline stage(s): 7 Order package + bracket, 9 Live management

**Why it's good at its job.** Once a trade is open, it re-checks five times where the price is likely to get to. Like a navigator who only extends the destination when the road ahead is clear, never shortens it. So it can add profit on strong trades without ever cutting a winner short.

| Stat | What | Provenance · kind · n / window |
|---|---|---|
| +0.0081R | Registered forward test, per trade: PASS | MEASURED · offline, forward · n 868 · Oct 2026 |
| +0.0008R | Live policy (extend only), per trade | MEASURED · offline replay · n 907 · 2025-10 → 2026-09 |
| -0.0141R | Exit head (close now?): FAIL | MEASURED · offline, out-of-sample · n 1041 · 3 folds 2022–2026 |

**What's next.** → Re-check it once 300 live trades have closed since 12 November. → Test checking in on market events, not only on a timer. → Exit head: confirm a new training label (+0.007R per trade, just under the bar) on a wider margin.


**Speaker notes.** Labrador manages a trade that is already open. At five check-ins it re-estimates how far the trade is likely to run and can only move the target further out, never closer, so it cannot cut a winner short. Its registered forward test passed and its live gain is small but positive. Its sibling, the exit head, decides whether to close now; it failed out of sample on two of three markets and only acts on the demo floor. A new training label for it showed a small gain that missed the pass bar, and the next version is queued.

## 11. Model card · Basset, the early-warning light (step 11)

Provenance: MEASURED

Pipeline stage(s): 11 Grading, evidence, research

**Why it's good at its job.** It watches every strategy's results against what its backtest promised, trade by trade. Like a smoke detector: small shortfalls add up, and when they build past a line it rings. Today it is a warning light, not an automatic switch, because the switch failed a fresh test.

| Stat | What | Provenance · kind · n / window |
|---|---|---|
| 0.654 | Spots a fading strategy (0.5 = coin flip): PASS | MEASURED · offline, in-window · AUC · n 664 points · 24 strategies |
| PASS | Stopping on the alarm beat random stops, in-window | MEASURED · offline, in-window · p 0.0115 · 654 skipped trades |
| FAIL | Same rule on a fresh window: did not replicate | MEASURED · offline, fresh window · p 0.994 · 620 skipped · before 2026-05 |

**What's next.** → Keep the alarm as a warning in reviews; it does not switch anything off. → Redesign the action before retesting, on data it has never seen. → Find out why AI grades of past trades ran opposite to their results before using them as a signal.


**Speaker notes.** The newest family, and an honest one to show. Basset's job is to spot a strategy that is starting to fade before the losses pile up. The first baseline is a classic statistical alarm, a CUSUM: it adds up how far each strategy's live results fall short of its backtest and rings when the shortfall builds up. It works as a warning, better than chance in its test window. But stopping strategies when it rang did not hold up on a fresh window of data, so it stays a warning light and does not act. Both results are recorded, which is exactly how the loop is meant to work.

## 13. How it runs automatically, in one slide

Provenance: MEASURED

Pipeline stage(s): 1 Data in, 2 Signal builders, 5 Per-account routing, 8 Venue execution, 10 Close and reconcile

- **Venues** — Crypto perpetual futures, US funds and futures, and prop-firm accounts. 11 of 14 accounts sending orders.
- **Two switches** — Account: live or dry run. Strategy: live or shadow. No hidden third switch; never self-halts.
- **Run by agents** — AI agent sessions do the research and reviews; 23 operator-granted mandates let evidence-backed decisions proceed.

> MEASURED: config/accounts.yaml, config/mandates.yaml; rules docs/CLAUDE-RULES-CANONICAL.md § Prime Directive, CLAUDE.md § The two execution gates; config/values_exclusions.yaml.


**Speaker notes.** The infrastructure, briefly. The trader runs around the clock on cloud servers, on crypto, US funds and futures venues, plus prop-firm accounts. 11 of 14 accounts are switched on to send orders. Exactly two switches decide whether something trades: one per account, one per strategy. It never switches itself off: failures are contained to one trade or job and retried. Day to day, AI agent sessions run the research, reviews and bookkeeping under 23 standing mandates the operator has granted; agents may propose a mandate, only the operator grants one. Real money excludes fossil-fuel funds by the operator's policy.
