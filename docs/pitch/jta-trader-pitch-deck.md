## 2. The one-minute version

- **It trades by itself** — Many small, rule-based strategies run around the clock on crypto, US exchange-traded funds and futures. Each trade has a stop-loss and a profit target from the moment it opens.
- **It proves before it risks** — A strategy reaches real money only after a historical test, charged with realistic trading costs, clears a pass mark that was written down before the test was run.
- **It keeps learning** — Failures go back into research, not the bin. AI agent sessions run the research, the reviews and the bookkeeping. A human operator sets strategy and risk policy.


**Speaker notes.** Three sentences to remember. It trades automatically. It tests ideas on history, with full costs, before risking money. And it keeps learning, because every trade and every test feeds the next round of research. The rest of the deck explains each of these.

## 3. How to read the numbers in this deck

- **R = one unit of risk** — Before a trade opens, it gets a stop-loss. The money lost if the stop is hit is 1R. A trade that makes +2R made twice what it risked. One that hits its stop is −1R. R lets us compare strategies of any size without quoting money.
- **Expectancy** — The average R per trade. +0.20R means that, on average, each trade earned a fifth of what it risked, after costs. Small positive expectancy, repeated over many trades, is what an edge looks like.
- **The two tags** — MEASURED: read straight from a backtest result or a config file. ESTIMATED: depends on an assumption (e.g. an allowance for slippage). If we could not verify a number, it is not in the deck.


**Speaker notes.** Two ideas make every later slide readable. First, R. We never quote money; we quote how many units of risk a trade made or lost. Second, the tags. A MEASURED number was read directly from a result file. An ESTIMATED number depends on an assumption, for example how much worse than expected our fills are.

## 4. The problem: most trading ideas do not survive contact with costs

- An idea that looks profitable on a chart is often an illusion. Three things usually kill it:
  - Costs: exchange fees, slippage (getting a worse price than expected) and funding charges.
  - Overfitting: rules tuned so tightly to the past that they describe noise, not a pattern.
  - Moving the goalposts: deciding what counts as success after seeing the result.
- Running a live book well is also hard: positions must be protected 24/7, and every close must be explained.
- JTA Trader's answer is to make discipline mechanical: the rules are code and records, not willpower.


**Speaker notes.** This is the core problem the project is built around. Lots of ideas look good on a chart. Most of them stop working once you charge fees and slippage, or once you test on data the idea was not tuned on. People also fool themselves: they pick the pass mark after seeing the result. The system is designed to make those mistakes hard to make.

## 8. The life of a trade

1. **Data** — Prices arrive from four broker connections, every bar, every market.
2. **Signal** — Each strategy checks its own rules on its own markets.
3. **Risk check** — Per-account limits decide yes/no and the size, in R.
4. **Order** — Sent with a stop-loss and a profit target already attached.
5. **Manage** — Brackets can move, but only on evidence.
6. **Close & grade** — Exit reason recorded; trade graded and fed back to research.


**Speaker notes.** Every trade follows the same path. Market data arrives. Strategies check their rules. If a rule fires, the risk manager decides whether this account may take it and how big it is. The order goes out with a stop and a target already attached. While it is open, management may move the brackets. When it closes, the reason is recorded and the trade is graded.

## 9. Every trade carries a real stop and a real target

- **Stop-loss from the start** — Every order rests at the broker with a protective stop. That stop defines 1R.
- **A real profit target** — The target is a prediction, not a placeholder. A target set so far away it can never fill counts as no target, and a checker flags it.
- **Evidence-backed management** — Moving a stop or a target mid-trade is allowed. Each kind of move is advice only until its own test, net of costs, says it helps.


**Speaker notes.** A rule the operator set: no fictional take-profits. Many systems put a target so far away it never triggers, which is a way of not having one. Here, each strategy's target must be a real prediction of where price is going, and compliance is checked by a script. Active management means the system may move either bracket while a trade is open, but each kind of move stays advice-only until its own cost-aware test passes.

## 10. Risk controls that never switch the system off

- **Sizing in R** — Each account risks a fixed fraction per trade, so one loss is always a known, small amount.
- **Two switches only** — Account: live or dry run. Strategy: live or shadow. There is no hidden third switch.
- **Daily drawdown brake** — Per account, when armed: past a day's loss limit, open trades stay protected and new ones are blocked until the reset.
- **Never self-halts** — Failures are contained to the one trade or job and retried; persistent problems raise a flag to the operator while the system keeps going.


**Speaker notes.** Risk is handled per trade, not by switching the whole system off. There are exactly two switches that decide whether something trades: one per account, one per strategy. The one sanctioned brake is a daily drawdown switch per account: once a day's loss reaches a set level, existing positions keep their stops and targets but no new trades open until the reset.

## 11. Backtesting, explained simply

1. **Replay history** — Run the exact rule over past prices, trade by trade.
2. **Charge full costs** — Fees, plus allowances for slippage and funding.
3. **Test out of sample** — Score it on time periods it was not tuned on, split into folds.
4. **Pass mark first** — The rule for passing is registered before the run, never after.

> The current pass mark for most legs: out-of-sample net R, after the full cost stack, must be above zero (rule RULE-D1-STAGE0-NET-OF-FULL-COST, registered 2026-09-22).


**Speaker notes.** A backtest replays history and asks: if we had followed this rule, what would have happened? The trap is that you can always find a rule that worked in the past. So three protections: charge the full cost of trading; judge only on data the rule was not tuned on, split into several separate time periods called folds; and write down the pass mark before running the test.

## 12. The promotion ladder: three stages, two gates

- **STAGE 0 · Backtest** — Is there an edge, net of all costs? Passes only by clearing a pass mark registered before the run.
- **STAGE 1 · Paper** — Do the mechanics work? 1–2 clean trades. Skipped when the execution path is already proven.
- **STAGE 2 · Real money** — Does it hold up live, with its mirror? A paper mirror copies every trade; net-negative after costs → demoted.

> A paper or live record never proves an edge; it checks mechanics and cost. Edge is decided offline, at Stage 0.


**Speaker notes.** This is how an idea becomes a real-money strategy. Stage 0 decides whether there is an edge, offline. Stage 1, paper trading, is not a performance test. It is a mechanical check: does the order go out at the right size, do the stop and target rest at the broker, does the close get recorded. One or two clean trades are enough. If the execution path is already proven, Stage 1 is skipped. Stage 2 is real money plus a paper mirror that copies it exactly; if the mirror turns net-negative after costs over its window, the strategy is demoted.

## 17. Costs are charged in full, and they matter

Provenance: MEASURED, ESTIMATED

- **Fees** — Each venue's trading fee is charged on every entry and exit in the backtest.
- **Slippage** — An allowance of 3–5 basis points per round trip for getting a worse price than the chart shows. ESTIMATED.
- **Funding** — Crypto perpetual futures pay or charge a periodic funding rate; the backtest charges an allowance for it. ESTIMATED.
- **The effect** — On the 6 production legs with a fee-only figure, slippage + funding cut 1%–28% of net R. ESTIMATED.

> Inputs MEASURED from comms/strategy_evidence/<leg>.json (net_r_oos vs net_r_oos_fee_only, cost_stack); the resulting range is ESTIMATED because the slippage and funding inputs are allowances.


**Speaker notes.** Why we insist on the full cost stack. Taking the production legs that have a fee-only number on file (n = 6 of 7 production legs), adding slippage and funding allowances removed between 1% and 28% of their fee-only net result. That range is ESTIMATED because the slippage allowance is an assumption, not a measurement of our fills. A strategy that only passes on fees alone does not pass.

## 20. Iterate on levers, not on luck

- Every strategy, ML model and live-trade action class goes through the same research workflow.
- The centre of it is understanding how each lever moves the outcome, not pass/fail alone.
  - Levers include entry timing, stop distance, target placement, market regime and timeframe.
- A failed test returns with a diagnosis and the next lever to test.
- Retirement needs evidence that no lever helps, plus a written test that would bring it back.
- Weekly programme reviews check that every non-passing strategy has a live next step.


**Speaker notes.** A rule the operator set on 1 November: when something fails, we do not just try a different random idea. We work out why it failed, identify the levers that could change the outcome, and test them one by one. A strategy is retired only when the levers are exhausted, never after a fixed number of attempts. The same applies to ML models and to the live-trade review.

## 21. Live books check mechanics and cost, never edge

- **Mechanics** — Did the signal fire, the order go out at the right size, the brackets rest at the broker, and the close get a real reason?
- **Cost** — Are real fees and slippage in line with the backtest's allowances?
- **Demotion signal** — The paper mirror's net-of-cost result over its declared window triggers demotion to paper.
- **Live-trade review** — An AI review of open trades recommends the best action now, each option priced from that strategy's own history.


**Speaker notes.** Live and paper trades are watched closely, but they do not decide whether a strategy has an edge; a handful of live trades is far too few. They check that the machinery works and that costs match what the backtest assumed. The paper mirror of each real-money book is the early warning: if it goes net-negative after costs over its window, the strategy moves back to paper.

## 23. Numbers you can trust

- **Provenance labels** — Every number is MEASURED, ESTIMATED, FABRICATED or UNVERIFIED, and the four are never mixed.
- **“0” vs “could not look”** — A count that reads zero must say whether we actually looked. Missing data shows as “—”.
- **Merged ≠ deployed ≠ observed** — A change is only done when its effect is seen on the running system, and reports say which of the three is true.
- **Automated guards** — Checks on every change keep rules, configuration and docs from drifting apart, e.g. a guard refuses a strategy on both paper and real money.


**Speaker notes.** Small rules that stop the most common reporting mistakes. Every number carries a label saying whether it was measured or estimated, or worse. A zero is never allowed to hide the fact that we could not look. A missing value shows as a dash, never as zero. And automated checks run on every change to keep the documents, the configuration and the code in agreement.
