## 2. The one-minute version

- **It trades by itself** — Many small, rule-based strategies run around the clock on crypto, US exchange-traded funds and futures. Each trade has a stop-loss and a profit target from the moment it opens.
- **It proves before it risks** — A strategy reaches real money only after a historical test, charged with realistic trading costs, clears a pass mark that was written down before the test was run. Then it goes straight to real money.
- **It keeps learning** — Failures go back into research, not the bin. AI agent sessions run the research, the reviews and the bookkeeping. A human operator sets strategy and risk policy.


**Speaker notes.** Three sentences to remember. It trades automatically. It tests ideas on history, with full costs, before risking money. And it keeps learning, because every trade and every test feeds the next round of research. The rest of the deck explains each of these.

## 3. How to read the numbers in this deck

- **R = one unit of risk** — Before a trade opens, it gets a stop-loss. The money lost if the stop is hit is 1R. A trade that makes +2R made twice what it risked. One that hits its stop is −1R. R lets us compare strategies of any size without quoting money.
- **Expectancy** — The average R per trade. +0.20R means that, on average, each trade earned a fifth of what it risked, after costs. Small positive expectancy, repeated over many trades, is what an edge looks like.
- **The two tags** — MEASURED: read straight from a backtest result or a config file. ESTIMATED: depends on an assumption (e.g. an allowance for slippage). If we could not verify a number, it is not in the deck.


**Speaker notes.** Two ideas make every later slide readable. First, R. We never quote money; we quote how many units of risk a trade made or lost. Second, the tags. A MEASURED number was read directly from a result file. An ESTIMATED number depends on an assumption, for example how much worse than expected our fills are.

## 4. The problem: most trading ideas do not survive contact with costs

- An idea that looks profitable on a chart is often an illusion. Three things usually kill it:
  - Costs: exchange fees, slippage (getting a worse price than expected) and funding charges.
  - Overfitting: rules tuned so tightly to the past that they describe noise, not a pattern.
  - Moving the goalposts: deciding what counts as success after seeing the result.
- Running a live book well is also hard: positions must be protected 24/7, and every close must be explained.
- JTA Trader's answer is to make discipline mechanical: the rules are code and records, not willpower.


**Speaker notes.** This is the core problem the project is built around. Lots of ideas look good on a chart. Most of them stop working once you charge fees and slippage, or once you test on data the idea was not tuned on. People also fool themselves: they pick the pass mark after seeing the result. The system is designed to make those mistakes hard to make.

## 8. The life of a trade

1. **Data** — Prices arrive from each broker connection, every bar, every market.
2. **Signal** — Each strategy checks its own rules on its own markets.
3. **Risk check** — Per-account limits decide yes/no and the size, in R.
4. **Order** — Sent with a stop-loss and a profit target already attached.
5. **Manage** — Brackets can move, but only on evidence.
6. **Close & grade** — Exit reason recorded; trade graded and fed back to research.


**Speaker notes.** Every trade follows the same path. Market data arrives. Strategies check their rules. If a rule fires, the risk manager decides whether this account may take it and how big it is. The order goes out with a stop and a target already attached. While it is open, management may move the brackets. When it closes, the reason is recorded and the trade is graded.

## 9. Every trade carries a real stop and a real target

Provenance: MEASURED

- **Stop-loss from the start** — Every order rests at the broker with a protective stop. That stop defines 1R.
- **A real profit target** — The target is a prediction, not a placeholder. A target set so far away it can never fill counts as no target, and a checker flags it.
- **Real money today** — Target at entry: 8 of 9 routings. Target revised in the trade: 1 of 9. Closing this gap is on the roadmap.

> MEASURED: scripts/ci/check_tp_doctrine.py, 85 routings over every account in config/accounts.yaml, commit d66f12260. A routing is one strategy on one account.


**Speaker notes.** A rule the operator set: no fictional take-profits. Many systems put a target so far away it never triggers, which is a way of not having one. Here, each strategy's target must be a real prediction of where price is going, and the prediction should keep moving with the trade. A script measures compliance on every routing of a strategy to an account. Honest status: on real money, 8 of 9 routings set a real target at entry, but only 1 of 9 also revise it during the trade. That gap is open work, and the checker only lets the list of exceptions shrink.

## 10. Active management: either bracket can move, on market evidence

- **The whole bracket** — Stop: tighten, widen or move to a structure level. Target: extend, pull in or re-estimate. Position: partial or full close. Or hold, which is recorded too.
- **Market-triggered** — Structure, volatility, momentum, regime, scheduled events or liquidity levels. Never P&L alone; never the clock alone.
- **Applied, then graded** — Reviewed actions are applied through each account's own risk checks, with a ping per change. Each action class is graded on its own rule: a small cost for a material cut in risk, holding across time and beating chance.


**Speaker notes.** Once a trade is open, the system may move the stop or the target, or close part or all of the position. The trigger must be something in the live market: structure, volatility, momentum, regime, a scheduled event. Never the trade's profit alone and never the clock alone. Both brackets always stay in place. Since 14 November, every action a reviewed recommendation names is applied through each account's own risk manager, and the operator gets a ping for each change. Each kind of action is still graded separately against a risk-adjusted, cost-aware rule.

## 11. Risk controls that never switch the system off

- **Sizing in R** — Each account risks a fixed fraction per trade, so one loss is always a known, small amount.
- **Two switches only** — Account: live or dry run. Strategy: live or shadow. There is no hidden third switch.
- **Daily drawdown brake** — Per account, when armed: past a day's loss limit, open trades stay protected and new ones are blocked until the reset.
- **Never self-halts** — Failures are contained to the one trade or job and retried; persistent problems raise a flag to the operator while the system keeps going.


**Speaker notes.** Risk is handled per trade, not by switching the whole system off. There are exactly two switches that decide whether something trades: one per account, one per strategy. The one sanctioned brake is a daily drawdown switch per account: once a day's loss reaches a set level, existing positions keep their stops and targets but no new trades open until the reset.

## 12. Backtesting, explained simply

1. **Replay history** — Run the exact rule over past prices, trade by trade.
2. **Charge full costs** — Fees, plus allowances for slippage and funding.
3. **Test out of sample** — Score it on time periods it was not tuned on, split into folds.
4. **Pass mark first** — The rule for passing is registered before the run, never after.

> The current pass mark for most legs: out-of-sample net R, after the full cost stack, must be above zero (rule RULE-D1-STAGE0-NET-OF-FULL-COST, registered 2026-09-22).


**Speaker notes.** A backtest replays history and asks: if we had followed this rule, what would have happened? The trap is that you can always find a rule that worked in the past. So three protections: charge the full cost of trading; judge only on data the rule was not tuned on, split into several separate time periods called folds; and write down the pass mark before running the test.

## 13. The promotion ladder: a backtest pass goes straight to real money

- **STAGE 0 · Backtest** — Is there an edge, net of all costs?. Passes only by clearing a pass mark registered before the run, on held-out data.
- **STAGE 2 · Real money** — Live, with a paper mirror. Entered directly on a Stage-0 pass. The mirror net-negative after costs → demoted.
- **STAGE 1 · Demo floor** — Paper trading, for data. Home of every testing leg, and of demoted legs while they are iterated on.

> No soak step between a backtest pass and real money (operator, 2026-11-12). A paper or live record never proves an edge; it checks mechanics and cost.


**Speaker notes.** This is how an idea becomes a real-money strategy. Stage 0 decides whether there is an edge, offline, on history, net of the full cost stack. Since 12 November there is no step in between: a strategy that passes Stage 0 goes straight to real money, with a paper mirror that copies it exactly. The first live trades are watched as a mechanical check, never as a gate. The demo floor, Stage 1, is where every strategy that has not passed yet trades on paper to generate data, and where a demoted strategy goes back to keep generating data while it is improved. If the mirror turns net-negative after costs over its window, the strategy is demoted to the floor.

## 14. What “pass” means for real money

- **Held-out data decides** — The chosen configuration must pass its pre-registered rule on data it was not tuned on, after fees, slippage and funding.
- **A committed record** — The result is written by a script into an evidence record in the repository. A claim in a message is not a record.
- **Probation, not a block** — The deflated Sharpe ratio is reported on every decision. A live strategy below 0.95 stays live but keeps iterating toward a version that clears it.


**Speaker notes.** Two clarifications on the pass mark. First, real money is governed by a held-out test: the chosen configuration must clear its registered rule on data it never saw during tuning, net of the full cost stack, written into a committed evidence record. Second, a stricter statistic, the deflated Sharpe ratio, which corrects for how many variants were tried, is reported on every promotion but does not block it. A live strategy below its bar is on probation: it keeps an open research unit whose goal is a version that clears it.

## 20. Costs are charged in full, and they matter

Provenance: MEASURED, ESTIMATED

- **Fees** — Each venue's trading fee is charged on every entry and exit in the backtest.
- **Slippage** — An allowance of 3–5 basis points per round trip for getting a worse price than the chart shows. ESTIMATED.

> Inputs MEASURED from comms/strategy_evidence/<leg>.json (net_r_oos vs net_r_oos_fee_only, cost_stack); the resulting range is ESTIMATED because the slippage and funding inputs are allowances.


**Speaker notes.** Why we insist on the full cost stack. Taking the production legs that have a fee-only number on file (n = 7 of 9 production legs), adding slippage and funding allowances removed between 2% and 51% of their fee-only net result. That range is ESTIMATED because the slippage allowance is an assumption, not a measurement of our fills. A strategy that only passes on fees alone does not pass.

## 23. Iterate on levers, not on luck

- Every strategy, ML model and live-trade action class goes through the same research workflow.
- The centre of it is understanding how each lever moves the outcome, not pass/fail alone.
  - Levers include entry timing, stop distance, target placement, market regime and timeframe.
- A failed test returns with a diagnosis and the next lever to test.
- Retirement needs evidence that no lever helps, plus a written test that would bring it back.
- Weekly programme reviews check that every non-passing strategy has a live next step.


**Speaker notes.** A rule the operator set on 1 November: when something fails, we do not just try a different random idea. We work out why it failed, identify the levers that could change the outcome, and test them one by one. A strategy is retired only when the levers are exhausted, never after a fixed number of attempts. The same applies to ML models and to the live-trade review.

## 24. Live books check mechanics and cost, never edge

- **Mechanics** — Did the signal fire, the order go out at the right size, the brackets rest at the broker, and the close get a real reason?
- **Cost** — Are real fees and slippage in line with the backtest's allowances?
- **Demotion signal** — The paper mirror's net-of-cost result over its declared window triggers demotion back to the demo floor.
- **Probation** — A live strategy whose deflated Sharpe ratio is below its bar keeps an open research unit to build a stronger version.


**Speaker notes.** Live and paper trades are watched closely, but they do not decide whether a strategy has an edge; a handful of live trades is far too few. They check that the machinery works and that costs match what the backtest assumed. The paper mirror of each real-money book is the early warning: if it goes net-negative after costs over its window, the strategy moves back to the demo floor, where it keeps trading on paper for data while it is improved.

## 27. Numbers you can trust

- **“0” vs “could not look”** — A count that reads zero must say whether we actually looked. Missing data shows as “—”.

**Speaker notes.** Small rules that stop the most common reporting mistakes. Every number carries a label saying whether it was measured or estimated, or worse. And automated checks run on every change to keep the documents, the configuration and the code in agreement.

> Map: docs/plans/ML-ROADMAP-MECE-2026-11.md. “Models with a consumer” = a strategy, account or config uses it (demo floor included), per the 2026-11-11 inventory (MEASURED, dated). Family names are internal labels.


**Speaker notes.** The ML plan cuts the life of a trade into ten decisions, from which markets to trade to grading a closed trade. Each decision is owned by exactly one model family, with one question, a baseline it must beat net of cost, a label, and an offline test. Each slot runs its own champion and challenger loop: the model in use is the champion, a retrained or new model is the challenger, and the challenger replaces it only by beating it on the same data and the same costs. Several slots have no model yet; they run on rules today, which is stated rather than hidden.

## 30. Roadmap 1 — scale the real-money strategies

- **A mechanical ladder** — Backtest pass → real money; mirror net-negative → back to the demo floor. Decided by registered rules and standing mandates.
- **More legs through Stage 0** — 50 testing legs keep iterating on their levers; each one that passes goes to real money.
- **New horizons** — Daily and weekly holds, and sub-hour breakout families, tested net of cost.

> Source: ROADMAP.md live block (generated from docs/roadmap/milestones.yaml), goal G1: L-AM-STANDARD, L-ATM, L-LADDER, L-TIMEFRAMES. Testing-leg count MEASURED.


**Speaker notes.** Goal one on the roadmap is real-money edge, decided offline. The near-term work: active management as a standard on every real-money strategy, with targets that are real predictions and move with the trade; a promotion ladder that runs mechanically from a backtest pass to real money and back; more testing legs pushed through Stage 0; and longer and shorter timeframes, always net of cost.

## 34. Appendix B — what an evidence record contains

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| coverage_state | measured · harness_failed · no_harness · not_attempted — read first |
| net_r_oos / expectancy_r_oos | Out-of-sample net R and R per trade, after the full cost stack |
| n_trades_oos · folds · folds_positive | Sample size and how many separate time folds were positive |
| cost_stack | Fees, slippage allowance, funding allowance, with the source run |
| fidelity · omitted_levers | faithful = every configured lever modelled; approximate names the gaps |
| decision_rule | Rule id, when it was registered, and the verdict (pass / fail) |
| path_stats | Max drawdown in R, worst trade, worst day — descriptive, not a verdict |

> Source: comms/strategy_evidence/README.md and the records themselves (producer scripts/ops/build_strategy_evidence.py).


**Speaker notes.** Each strategy has one evidence record, written by a script, never by hand. These are the fields that matter. Coverage state is read first, so a missing number is never mistaken for a bad one. Fidelity says whether the harness modelled every setting the live strategy uses.

## 36. Appendix D — glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| R | One unit of risk: what a trade loses if its stop-loss is hit |
| Expectancy | Average R per trade, after costs |
| Leg | One strategy on one market and timeframe |
| Out of sample / fold | Data the rule was not tuned on; a fold is one separate time slice of it |
| Slippage | Getting a worse price than expected when the order fills |
| Funding | Periodic payment between long and short holders of crypto perpetual futures |
| Paper / mirror | Simulated-money account at the broker / a paper copy of a real-money book |
| Mandate | A standing operator grant to act on a class of decision when the evidence settles it |
| Prop firm | A firm that funds traders under strict loss limits |
| Demo floor | The paper accounts where every testing or demoted strategy trades for data |
| Held-out | Data kept aside and never used for tuning; the real-money pass is judged on it |
| Champion / challenger | The model in use vs a new one; the new one replaces it only by beating it on the same data |


**Speaker notes.** Plain definitions of the terms used in the deck.
